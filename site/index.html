Two out of three is not enough

In 2006, astronomers meeting in Prague voted on what makes a planet. About 2,000 of them were supposed to vote. Most had already gone home. The 400 who stayed decided for everyone. Three rules. Pluto met two.

The Leader has seen the numbers. It was rigged.

Centuries later, the Human Confederation rules the Solar System. Planets get a seat, a vote and their own ice. Dwarfs pay tax.

The Great Gravitas, Pluto's Leader, wants the title back. He reads "clear the neighbourhood" as housekeeping and blames the Kuipers, migrants from the Belt, for everything. The Kuipers are the ones keeping the colony alive.

What the game is

Make Pluto Great Again is a single-player colony-building and management game, seen in isometric view, set on a frozen Pluto.

You play a civil servant at the Department of Orbital Hygiene. You don't control anyone directly. You place the buildings and the colony runs itself: Plutonians and Kuipers cut the ice, haul it to the depots and build whatever you place.

Then the Leader sends a decree. Decrees are not written with your colony in mind. You decide what to do with each one, and you live with the consequences.

Leaders are to be followed. Civil servants who disagree are reassigned. Nobody has seen where.

The goal

Make Pluto heavy enough to be a planet again.

Cut ice and feed it to the Mass Accretor, block by block. The Planethood meter tracks Pluto's mass against what it needs to clear its neighbourhood. Get there and the astronomers have to vote again.

And don't let your colony die.

Plutonians and Kuipers are built from Pluto itself: bones of water ice, blood of liquid nitrogen. It flows between 63 and 77 kelvin (−210 to −196 °C, −346 to −321 °F). Pluto is 40 kelvin (−233 °C, −388 °F). Out in the open a colonist slows down, then freezes solid, and if nobody warms them up, they die. Put heaters near the work and hab domes near the heaters. No free beds, no new arrivals.

You lose if:

  • your loyalty hits zero and you are "reassigned" (fudged reports help, until an audit catches them);
  • the colony is lost: no colonists left, or no Ministry.

How to play

  1. Build

    Place ice quarries, depots, heaters, hab domes and the Mass Accretor. Keep everyone warm: a colonist left in the cold freezes to death.

  2. Let it run

    Colonists pick up the jobs on their own. Watch the ice flow, find the bottlenecks, fix them.

  3. Handle the decree

    Obey and the colony takes the hit. Obey on paper and the numbers drift apart. Refuse and your loyalty drops.

  4. Meet the quota

    Every few shifts the Ministry reports Pluto's official mass. Miss the Leader's quota and you lose loyalty. Obeying on paper inflates the official figure. The real one is in the report too.

Windows 10 or later, 64-bit

  1. Unzip the .zip file.
  2. Run MakePlutoGreatAgain.exe.

If Windows says "Windows protected your PC", click More info, then Run anyway.

macOS

Apple Silicon and Intel

  1. Unzip the file and drag Make Pluto Great Again into Applications.
  2. The first time, macOS will say the app "is damaged" or "can't be opened". It isn't damaged: it just isn't notarized by Apple. Open Terminal and run: 
    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Make Pluto Great Again.app"
  3. Open it as usual.

FAQ

When can I play it?

When it's ready. It's early. Very early. Come back soon.

Why do Windows and macOS warn me about it?

Because the game isn't signed with a paid certificate from Microsoft or Apple. That's normal for small independent games. The steps above get you past the warning.

Is it about real politicians?

No. It's a satire of scapegoating politics, and everyone in it is invented: the Leader, the Plutonians, the Kuipers and every slogan. The joke is always on the regime, never on the people it blames.

Is there combat?

No. Your weapons are ice, forms and creative accounting.
