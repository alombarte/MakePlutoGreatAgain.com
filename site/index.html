Two out of three is not enough.

In 2006, astronomers meeting in Prague voted on what makes a planet. About 2,000 of them were supposed to vote. Most had already gone home. The 400 who stayed decided for everyone. Three rules. Pluto met two.

The Leader has seen the numbers. It was rigged.

Centuries later, the Human Confederation rules the Solar System. Planets get a seat, a vote and their own ice. Dwarfs pay tax.

The Great Gravitas, Pluto's Leader, wants the title back. He reads "clear the neighbourhood" as housekeeping and blames the Kuipers, migrants from the Belt, for everything. The Kuipers are the ones keeping the colony alive.

They voted. We counted. Again.

You cut ice, haul it to the depots and feed the Mass Accretor, block by block, until Pluto is heavy enough to count. Then the decrees arrive.

Every decree gives you three options. Obey, and pay the price. Obey on paper, and hope nobody audits the report. Or refuse, and watch your loyalty drop.
